# Audit — 2026-08-07

Three independent reviewers: fresh eyes on the week's incident-driven fixes,
an adversarial pass over the stable money paths, and a prior-art researcher
answering "for each bug we hit live, how does the field solve it — and are our
fixes standard, better, or idiosyncratic?" All read-only; every HIGH was
demonstrated with a runnable script against the real modules before it counted.

All ten HIGHs are fixed with specs that fail against the old behaviour
(PR #83). Suite: **306 specs**, up from 303.

## The meta-finding: fix-here-not-there

Most of these HIGHs were **incomplete ports of already-diagnosed findings** —
the fix landed in one module while a sibling path kept the same assumption:

- R8 ("a forced close is a fill") was fixed in the readout; the bot's own
  reinvest/cooldown fills filter kept dropping unlinked closes.
- M15 closed the restart hole for the round *anchor*; the round *verdict*
  (M14) stayed process memory and re-entered after an unseen liquidation.
- M11 fixed trail-arming; the M10 guard band quietly reintroduced it.
- The Telegram anti-wedge fix protected the channel by dropping the very
  kill page the original finding was about.

New review obligation, effective now: **when a bug is diagnosed, every
sibling path that shares its assumption is audited in the same pass** — the
fix is not done when the firing path is patched.

## Wrong specs — rewritten

1. The M12/M13 fixtures gave closing TP fills **links** — inputs that cannot
   occur on the hosted venue those specs describe. Green suite, impossible
   inputs; that is how HIGH #1 hid.
2. The M10 spec pinned "near-miss = fired" — the harmful semantics of HIGH #8,
   asserted as correct.
3. The R9 spec claimed truncation immunity that a demonstrated case falsifies,
   and tested only the trivially-correct cases.

## Prior art: how the field handles our bug classes

A third researcher surveyed freqtrade, Hummingbot, OctoBot, Nautilus Trader,
passivbot, ccxt's manual, two tax-accounting engines, and seven exchanges'
own docs. Verdicts:

- **Fill-list lag** — *standard in structure, stricter in one respect.* The
  exchanges admit the lag in writing (Binance tiers its REST endpoints by
  data-source freshness; Bybit ships a WS sequence token because fills and
  positions are observed out of sync). Nobody surveyed prices state from the
  fills list; Nautilus names our exact race as a config parameter. Our
  cover-the-holding refusal has no direct precedent — where Nautilus
  *fabricates* inferred fills, we refuse.
- **Close attribution** — *finer-grained than the best surveyed system.*
  Nautilus converged on the same design but its claims are side-blind; our
  (market, symbol, side) tuple handles the hedged pair it cannot. Hummingbot
  silently drops unrecognised fills; freqtrade forbids the problem (one
  subaccount per bot).
- **Spot cost basis** — *one direct precedent.* passivbot v6 spot did almost
  exactly G15's coverage walk — but does not refuse on truncated history,
  silently yielding a wrong basis where we withhold exits. Refuse-when-
  uncovered is matched only by Hummingbot's opt-in inventory-cost mode and
  the rp2 tax engine.
- **Fee-unaware churn** — *better than everything surveyed.* Only KuCoin
  *enforces* profit-above-fees; every surveyed OSS engine is fee-blind at
  build time (one ships a default config that loses ~15 bps per round trip
  and builds without a murmur). R9's live zero-spread ratio has **no
  analogue anywhere** — everyone else finds fee churn in the accounting,
  after the money is gone.
- **Degraded reads** — *standard practice confirmed.* No mature system acts
  irreversibly on one empty read; freqtrade contains a literal two-read
  confirm. The v2 behaviour we fixed has no precedent in any mature system.
- **Idempotent placement** — *textbook.* Assume-placed-then-reconcile matches
  Binance's and ccxt's first-party guidance nearly word for word; both major
  OSS bots do worse (one re-enters after a timed-out entry that landed, the
  other orphans it).

**Adoption list, ranked** (queued as backlog items): deterministic client
order IDs on market/seed writes (makes E6 reconciliation an exact query);
net sells inside the G15 coverage walk (passivbot's signed-qty variant —
keep our refusal, take their arithmetic); a temporal deadband before
believing venue discrepancies (Nautilus: no discrepancy counts until N ms
after our own last write); consider KuCoin's hard block for fee-losing grids
(currently a warn, B8-style — owner's call); confirm explicit-zero position
rows get the same E9 treatment as empty lists; long-term, private WebSocket
as authority with REST as backup (post-promotion, not this pass).

## What this changes about the build

1. **Fix-here-not-there is a review obligation** (above).
2. **Fixture realism is spec correctness.** Two of three wrong specs were
   wrong because their *fixtures* described a venue that does not exist.
   A spec's inputs are claims too.
3. **The evidence bar held.** Every HIGH arrived with a demonstration; the
   two suspicions that couldn't be demonstrated were filed as MED and two of
   them dissolved on investigation. Demonstrate-or-downgrade stays.
